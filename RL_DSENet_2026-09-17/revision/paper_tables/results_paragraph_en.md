# Results paragraph (requires the stated protocol and limitations)

Under a common executed basal-rate envelope of the observed warm-up rate ±0.25 U/h, DSENet-RL achieved a time in range (70–180 mg/dL) of 97.08 ± 4.50%, compared with 89.78 ± 8.77% for FQL, the strongest external comparator on this endpoint. The patient-paired difference was 7.30 percentage points (95% patient-bootstrap CI, 2.62–12.44; Holm-adjusted two-sided exact sign-flip p=0.0273 across eight external comparisons). However, time below 70 mg/dL increased from 0.66% to 1.55%, and time below 54 mg/dL increased from 0.25% to 0.57%. No method in the common-envelope evaluation terminated early. These findings indicate a tradeoff between time in range and hypoglycemia rather than uniformly superior control.

All methods were evaluated in the same 60 scenarios comprising ten previously exposed virtual adults, two meal seeds, and three shared meal-bolus conditions. Outcomes were averaged over six scenarios per patient and then equally over patients; reported standard deviations describe between-patient variation. Each trainable method used one training seed, so statistical intervals do not capture training-seed uncertainty. The proposed system additionally used inherited simulation-trained context and paired simulator supervision, while the external offline baselines used Loop data only. The comparison therefore evaluates control systems under a shared evaluation protocol, not algorithmic superiority under identical training information. Common clipping also does not remove differences in discrete versus continuous policy support or explicit anchor inputs.
